The thirty-second version

A narrative RPG on a genuinely broken world.

The world literally broke into nine chunks. Great news for anyone who hates a normal map.

Sky of Tides has the two things a narrative RPG has to have: a world you want to walk around in and a shape you can commit to. Nine floating realms above a molten inner sun is the kind of setting most games hint at once and back away from. This one lives in it. Nine voiced episodes give it a runtime we can plan around. The whole thing works if the risk and discovery choices leave marks.

Okay, what is this thing?

The world is called numen9. It is nine floating realms above a molten inner sun, and if that sentence didn't already sell it to you, we can't help you. Rin D'Lorah is our protagonist. She travels between the realms, has real choices to make about who she is in each one, and (crucially) is fully voiced across all nine episodes. That episode structure is the reason we can actually imagine finishing this.

The system pitch is that dialogue, discovery, and risk choices all leave marks on Rin and on the realms she's moving through. That's the promise. The reality has to be that a choice made in episode two is still audible in episode seven. Most narrative RPGs quietly forget you shifted an alliance the moment the next arc begins. If Sky of Tides remembers, it's the kind of thing our group chat argues about for a month. If it doesn't, it's still a nice place to visit.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • The world numen9 is nine floating realms above a molten inner sun.
  • The story is told across nine fully voiced episodes.
  • Dialogue, discovery, and risk choices are described as leaving marks on Rin and the world.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Episodes let me breathe.

I'm not going to sit down for a forty-hour campaign. I am happy to sit down for one voiced episode and work out whether I want a second. That episode shape is the reason I'll open the game at all. My one worry is lore density. If the first hour drops thirty proper nouns on me, I'll politely close the tab. If the first episode teaches me gently, I'm staying.

02

The Descent Guy

A choice is only a choice if something breaks.

Narrative RPGs love saying 'your choices matter.' I love calling them on it. If picking a side in episode two closes a door in episode six, that's a real system. If picking a side changes one line of dialogue and everything else proceeds identically, the game's theater. I'll have worked out which one this is by episode three, and I'll tell the other two in unnecessary detail.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Nine chapters I can actually schedule.

Full-voiced episodes with clear breakpoints are exactly the RPG structure I stick with. I can play one on a Sunday, put it down for a week, come back and pick it up. My concern is whether the world holds together across the pauses. If each episode's memorable enough to text about, I finish the whole thing. If they blur, I lose the thread at chapter five.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Narrative RPG fans who care about world first and stats second
  • Players who need genuine episode breaks to finish a story
  • Anyone who reads 'molten inner sun' and immediately wants to visit

Dad caveats

  • Terminology density in the first episode
  • Whether an early choice is still visible five episodes later
  • Pacing across nine chapters, with no soft middle
