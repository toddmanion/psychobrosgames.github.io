The thirty-second version

Peppino is our new life coach.

Best cardio we've done in a decade and we didn't have to leave the couch.

Pizza Tower is 90s cartoon cocaine in game form. The movement snaps in a way we haven't felt since the Genesis, the pixel art is on some ungovernable next level, and by hour four all three of us were making the escape screams. If you can handle a game that never stops yelling, it's an easy recommendation.

Okay, what is this thing?

Peppino Spaghetti sprints across a level flapping his arms like he's been personally attacked by a wasp, and this is the game's neutral state. Then a laser eyeball starts chasing him and he goes even faster. It's the funniest use of pixel art any of us has seen in years and the animation frames alone are worth the money.

Under the visual noise there's a really good platformer. Every level is a run to the top, then a timed escape back down, and the whole thing scores you on speed and combo chains. Wall jumps, super jumps, shoulder charges, taunts — none of it feels like homework and all of it feeds into the same 'go faster' loop. It's the rare score-attack game we actually re-ran on purpose.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Every level ends with a timed escape sequence that you sprint back through the geometry you just explored.
  • The pixel art is the work of a very small team and it shows in a way that's incredibly flattering to them.
  • Peppino has more animation frames than most AAA protagonists and about a hundred of them are just 'panicking'.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Even I was doing sick combos by the second level.

I usually watch Sonic and ask who Dr. Robotnik is. Not here. Within twenty minutes I'd learnt the shoulder charge, forgotten what the buttons were called, and cleared a whole floor without looking at the screen properly. Apparently my face when I pulled off the escape run for the first time was the best thing the other two saw all month.

02

The Descent Guy

The escape run is a subway map. My whole language.

The escape route is a puzzle you memorise, which is my exact love language. I got the highest scores in the group by treating each level like a subway map, plotting where to sprint and where to shoulder-charge. My only complaint is that the game rewards commitment more than precision, which I consider a moral failing. I was, by all accounts, insufferable about it for a week.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A game about panic that respects your session length.

Levels are five to ten minutes each and you can stop between any of them, which is a miracle for us. It's the loudest game in our library and we can only play it while the kids are asleep or the neighbours are on holiday, but the actual sessions are dad-sized. I beat a boss with twenty minutes left before bed and it made my whole week.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone whose favourite Wario Land is 'the one with the transformations'
  • Score-attack fans who hate being made to feel stupid about it
  • Group chats that need a new source of screenshots of a sweaty man

Dad caveats

  • You will need headphones and a household that's tolerant of screaming
  • P ranks are their own hobby and none of us have the wrists
  • The final act pushes the platforming hard enough that the Casual bounced

Receipts

Two of us cleared the main game on PC over the course of a month, one of us hit around six hours before life won. Facts checked against the game's official Steam page.
