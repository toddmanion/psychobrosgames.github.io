The thirty-second version

Arkane at Arkane again.

The only sim where 'I already know how tomorrow ends' is bragging, not depression.

DEATHLOOP is the Arkane immersive sim as a puzzle. You've got four times of day, four districts, and eight targets to kill in one loop. The joy is figuring out how. The wobble is that once you've solved it, the Julianna PvP invasion metagame is more of an idea than a scene. Worth playing anyway for the level design alone.

Okay, what is this thing?

You wake up on a beach in a nice jacket. You die. You wake up on the beach again. Someone called Julianna is on the radio being extremely rude to you. Colt has one day to kill eight targets across four maps, and if he can't get them all in a single loop, the loop resets and he has to figure out how to line them up. It's a puzzle box. A very stylish one.

What makes it work is that the map states genuinely change across morning, noon, afternoon and evening. A door that's locked in one is open in another. A target who's in one place at noon is at a party at night. You gather clues by playing badly, then plan a perfect loop with everything you've learned. When it clicks it's some of the smartest level design we've played since Dishonored 2, which is not an accident.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Four maps, four times of day, eight visionaries to kill in a single loop.
  • Powers are 'infused' with residuum you collect from previous runs, so nothing is truly lost.
  • Julianna can be a real player invading your loop, or an AI if you'd rather sleep.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I wanted a shooter and got a Rubik's cube with slabs.

I got maybe six hours in and started drifting. The problem is that DEATHLOOP wants you to hold a whole day of pieces in your head, and after work I don't have the head-room for that. I respected it. I want to be clear that I respected it. I also uninstalled it and put a hat on.

02

The Descent Guy

Four maps that talk to each other, and I drew a diagram.

I made a spreadsheet of which visionary is where at which time of day, colour-coded by lead. I solved the whole loop in my head before pulling a single trigger. Then I did it again, faster, without Reprise. I call that fun. The other two have quietly decided not to correct me. The level design is the closest thing to Dishonored 2 I've had in a decade.

03

Normal-ish Dad

One glorious campaign, and a metagame I waved goodbye to.

I finished it in about twenty hours and then closed it forever. Not because it's bad, but because I'm done. The Julianna invasions are a great idea and a small part of the pie, and I was never going to be the guy who lived in them. As a one-and-done immersive sim it's easily worth the money, and the flick knife twist ending is genuinely great.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Dishonored fans who wanted more Dishonored, please, thank you
  • Puzzle brains who want the puzzle to be 'plan a perfect day'
  • Anyone who reads investigation notes for fun in Skyrim

Dad caveats

  • The PvP invasion metagame is a small idea in a big frame
  • Colt won't shut up, and the constant chatter is a taste thing
  • On a controller some of the shooting feels a step behind the plan

Receipts

Two of us finished the main loop and rolled credits on PC, roughly twenty hours each; the third tried invasions for an afternoon and was fine with never doing so again.
