The thirty-second version

The best game about being terrified underwater.

First game to make me buy a bigger monitor. Then regret it, immediately.

Subnautica is the only survival game we've all finished, because it has an actual story and it respects your time. The base building is generous, the crafting is nested but sane, and the fear of the deep is completely earned. Some of us can't play it at all. Others can't play anything else. Both of these are correct responses.

Okay, what is this thing?

The escape pod hits the water. You surface. There is nothing on the horizon except a spectacularly on-fire spaceship and a very convincing sunset. You dip your head under and it is beautiful, and something down there sings a note you do not like. You don't know it yet, but that is the game telling you the ground floor is at about two hundred metres and there is no first floor.

The loop is proper survival: scan things, craft tools, build a base, make a bigger vehicle, go deeper. The Seamoth becomes the Cyclops becomes the PRAWN suit, and each unlock buys you access to a new biome that wants you dead in a specific new way. There are almost no guns. You mostly solve problems by running away. This is a survival game that understands its own horror.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • No procedural generation. The entire ocean is hand-built and hides the same secrets for everyone.
  • A full crafting and base-building tree, with power management, hull integrity, and modular subs.
  • There's a story, an actual ending, and a suggested critical path. Rare for the genre.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I uninstalled after my first Reaper sighting.

I stayed in the Safe Shallows for about four hours, built a modest base, farmed peepers, and had a lovely time. Then I saw a Reaper on the horizon, uninstalled the game, and told my kids I'd read a bad review. That bad review was the other two. They wrote it for me at the kitchen table. I'm still not okay.

02

The Descent Guy

Silent running, thermal reactors, and one deeply organised sub.

I kitted the Cyclops with silent running, thermal reactors, and a sub-frame's worth of storage lockers. I drew my own map of the biomes. I caught myself using words like 'logistics chain' about a lifepod, and neither of the others stopped me. This is my exact genre, and I couldn't be happier about how it turned out.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Forty-five hours, well spent, no regrets.

I finished it over about forty-five hours on PC and I think it's the best-paced survival game I've played. Sessions bank cleanly, the fear is real but not gratuitous, and the ending is properly earned. I don't recommend it to friends who are afraid of the water, and everybody who's ever done a dive with me thanks me for that.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Survival-curious players who bounce off crafting for its own sake
  • Anyone who liked FTL's 'you against the void' energy but wanted more scuba
  • Dads with a couple of hours after bedtime and nothing to prove

Dad caveats

  • Thalassophobia is not a joke here, and some of you should not play this
  • Performance can be rough in the late game, especially in the deepest biomes
  • You will hear the Reaper Leviathan noise in your dreams. That's the game working correctly.

Receipts

Two of us finished the main story on PC across around forty hours each; the third made it as far as the first Reaper and hasn't been back.
