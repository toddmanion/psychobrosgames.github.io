The thirty-second version

The co-op game they don't make anymore.

Sold as marriage counselling. It's actually kart racing. Same thing, honestly.

It Takes Two is a two-player co-op adventure that changes what it is every twenty minutes. Platformer, shooter, boss rush, minigolf, tower defence, sometimes in the same level. There is no solo mode and there shouldn't be. Play it with the person you live with, on the sofa, at a sensible bedtime, and it's genuinely one of the best things any of us have ever finished together.

Okay, what is this thing?

You start as two puppets. Your daughter cries on you. A magic self-help book with a mediterranean accent shows up. What follows is roughly twelve hours of one of you shouting 'no, YOUR side, YOUR side' at the other, punctuated by boss fights against household objects that had no business being that scary. We laughed. We were also asked why we were laughing at 11pm.

The mechanic is that each of you has a different toolkit every level, and they only work when combined. One gets nails, the other gets a hammer. One gets fire, the other gets sap. It never repeats itself. Just when you're getting good at one thing, Josef Fares yanks it away and hands you a whole new pair of powers. It shouldn't work as often as it does.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Two-player co-op only. There is no solo mode, and it's better for it.
  • Friend's Pass lets one copy invite a second player in for free.
  • Every level introduces a new pair of abilities and mostly retires them by the next.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The person who doesn't play games completed this with me.

My wife, who once described Mario Kart as 'the noisy one', finished this with me in three sittings. That's the point. The controls are simple enough that whichever half of a couple normally reads the instructions never has to. She got weirdly good at one boss I'd been dreading. I'm still processing that.

02

The Descent Guy

It refuses to let me settle in and I'm furious about it in a good way.

I had a whole plan for the first hour, and then the game handed me time-rewinding on a mouse. I kept expecting one core loop and got twelve small ones instead. This is not my usual thing. I've still ranked the boss fights, and I won't stop talking about the Rose's Room chapter, which coming from me is a compliment.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Finishable at the rate real adults play at.

Twelve to fifteen hours, split across four evenings, no filler, no side content nagging at me. My marriage remained intact. The story is silly and the ending genuinely earns its swing. If you can get two adults on the same sofa on the same night in the same year, this is what you should be putting on.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Couples with one gamer and one gamer-adjacent
  • Anyone who misses split-screen and forgives EA for the split-screen
  • Groups of two friends who need to prove they can still finish something together

Dad caveats

  • The dialogue leans hard on the therapy metaphor and lands where it lands
  • Zero solo option, so this genuinely needs a second human
  • Some late levels ask more of the platforming than we'd remembered

Receipts

Played to completion by two of us on separate PC sessions with our respective spouses on the sofa, roughly a week each.
