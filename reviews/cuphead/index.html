The thirty-second version

A masterpiece and a punishment. Same thing.

We had to start doing actual wrist stretches to play a cartoon.

Cuphead is one of the most beautiful games ever made and also the reason two of us stopped playing together for a month. The boss rush structure is glorious in short bursts and merciless if you sit down to grind. If you like precision platformers and can accept dying to a Rube Goldberg machine, buy it. If not, watch a good YouTube run and thank us.

Okay, what is this thing?

The first time you see the potato-plant boss you'll laugh, take a screenshot, and send it to a friend. Around the tenth time you're staring at the potato because you cannot get past his second phase, and it is midnight, and you have a stand-up meeting in six hours. Cuphead does not care. Cuphead is a 1930s cartoon, and 1930s cartoons hated you.

Mechanically it's a run-and-gun boss rush with three hits per attempt, a parry that requires you to jump a second time on a pink object, and a super meter you build by shooting. Every boss is hand-drawn, cel-animated, and has three or four phases you have to memorise. There is no assist mode. There is a Simple difficulty, and it does not count for the last island. That's the deal.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Every animation frame is hand-drawn on paper, in the style of Fleischer and early Disney shorts.
  • The soundtrack is original 1930s-style jazz, recorded live with a real band. It's not a filter.
  • Simple difficulty exists, but you need Regular clears to unlock the final island.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Two islands in and out, no regrets.

I got to the bee, laughed at the sting animation, died fourteen times, and traded the controller for a beer. I came back and beat exactly one more boss on a different night. That's where my Cuphead ends, and where I insist it should end for every dad, which is the closest I've come to writing a review of anything.

02

The Descent Guy

I know every parry window in the game.

I know every parry window in the game and I can list bosses in the order you should tackle them for the shop upgrades. I play with a lap-desk. I hum the level themes. I'm a menace but a joy, and this is exactly the kind of game my brain lives in when nobody's watching.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The forty-minute session is the correct dose.

I finished it on Regular over about fifteen hours because Cuphead sessions can be exactly forty minutes long, which is a huge point in its favour. I wouldn't recommend it to anyone whose hands hurt, or to anyone who doesn't already like precision platformers. I'd recommend it to everyone else, loudly, and I do.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • People who like Contra, Contra Hard Corps, and being humbled
  • Anyone with a soft spot for 1930s animation and jazz
  • Short-window players who want a discrete goal per session

Dad caveats

  • No assist mode, and Simple difficulty locks off the last island
  • Local co-op is available but arguably makes some bosses harder, not easier
  • Your kids will absolutely want to watch, and then they'll want to play

Receipts

Two of us finished it on Regular over the course of a year of on-and-off evenings on PC; the third quit at the honeybee and has stayed quit.
