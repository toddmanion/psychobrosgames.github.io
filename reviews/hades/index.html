The thirty-second version

Supergiant's masterpiece. Yes, we said it.

It's a game about screaming at your father, and unlike our teenage years, this time it's productive.

Hades is the game we hand people who say they don't like roguelikes. Every run tells a story, every death moves the plot forward, and the combat is stylish enough to carry an evening on its own. It's a rare confluence of writing, art, music and design where each one is doing the heavy lifting for the others. It's also one of the funniest games any of us has played.

Okay, what is this thing?

You're Zagreus. Your dad, Hades, runs the underworld and is, on a personal level, a piece of work. You'd like to leave. To leave, you have to fight your way up through four increasingly annoyed regions, take a boon from Zeus, Aphrodite, Ares or whoever's on the phone at the moment, and probably die at the last boss. Then you come back home, get shouted at by Hades, and try again. The next attempt starts about eight seconds later.

The core loop is that death is the only way to progress the story. Every run you fail unlocks new dialogue, new relationships, new upgrades from the mirror in your bedroom, and new pact conditions for the next attempt. Six weapons — sword, spear, shield, bow, fists, rail — each with wildly different Aspects, keep the combat fresh over dozens of runs. And every character in the underworld remembers what you said to them last.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • There are six weapons, each with several Aspects that meaningfully change how they play.
  • The story unfolds specifically through failed runs — you cannot skip it by being good.
  • Dying is roughly a ten-second inconvenience before you're back at the door.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The rare roguelike that never made me feel bad.

I grabbed the shield on my first run, liked how it made me basically invincible if I got the block timing right, and never once tried another weapon. I've beaten the last boss. I have no idea who Skelly is. I romanced nobody. I had a wonderful time. That's the whole point of Hades — it lets me do that without ever making me feel like I did it wrong.

02

The Descent Guy

The purest boon-stacking build game in the genre.

I read the codex. I learned which duo boons need which pairings. I've got opinions about Aspect of Guan Yu on the spear at high Heat. I beat the game on the sword first, then the spear, then every other weapon, then started the pact conditions, and I'd like you to know that Zeus's chain lightning breaks the game in the right hands. I'm not wrong. I'm also insufferable. Both true.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The perfect this-week-and-next-week game.

A run takes twenty to forty minutes, and there's always something new to unlock or somebody new to talk to when I die. I picked it up for a fortnight over a summer, put an hour in most evenings, and finished the credits with a stupid grin. It's the rare roguelike that rewards putting it down, because when I came back the story remembered where I was.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • People who bounced off other roguelikes and thought it was them
  • Anyone who's ever wanted to yell at their dad but has been quite well brought up
  • Fans of the Supergiant back catalogue who trust them at this point

Dad caveats

  • The credits are the middle of the game, not the end
  • You will absolutely start a run 'just for the mirror upgrades' and lose ninety minutes
  • Skelly cares about you and it's fine, don't overthink it

Receipts
