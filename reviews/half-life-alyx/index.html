The thirty-second version

Yes, buy the headset.

The scariest thing in VR isn't the headcrabs, it's admitting to your wife how much the headset cost.

Half-Life: Alyx is Valve at their absolute best, and it's the only game any of us has willingly bought a headset for. The gravity gloves alone are worth the price of the ticket. It's genuinely spooky in places, mechanically brilliant throughout, and it is a shame you cannot play it any other way than in VR, because you should absolutely play it.

Okay, what is this thing?

You are Alyx Vance. You are somewhere in City 17. There's a Combine patrol nearby and you have to physically duck down to hide from it. Then you rummage through a drawer, physically, for pistol rounds. Then a headcrab lunges at you and you shoot it in the face and shout something that used to be your dad's phrase and now belongs to you.

The gravity gloves are the whole thing. You point at an object, flick your wrist, and it comes to your hand across a room. It sounds small. It is not small. Every puzzle, every combat encounter, every quiet corridor is built around this one verb, and it makes the entire game feel like you're doing magic. Add in the best physical gunplay in VR and a story that fits neatly into Half-Life canon, and you have a genuine masterpiece.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • It's VR only — no flat-screen mode is officially supported.
  • Set between Half-Life and Half-Life 2, you play as Alyx Vance.
  • The gravity gloves are the mobility, puzzle and inventory system all in one.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The most I've ever played VR in one sitting.

I don't do VR. I get motion sick on a swivel chair. I put the headset on, played the first chapter for about half an hour, took the headset off looking pale, and said, and I'm quoting myself here, 'that was the most cinema anything's ever been for me.' I didn't finish it. I watched one of the others finish it. That counts.

02

The Descent Guy

The single best-designed VR mechanic to date.

I spent about forty minutes on the gravity gloves alone, flicking magazines into my hand from across rooms. I called it 'the first honest VR affordance,' which sounds insufferable and also is completely right. I then treated every combat encounter like a physics puzzle. I beat the Jeff sequence without ever making a sound. We are not discussing my high-school theatre background.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A masterpiece with an equipment tax.

It's around twelve hours long, sessions can be shorter than you'd think because your face gets sweaty, and the game demands a setup that not every household can support. I borrowed a headset for a fortnight, played it in ninety-minute stretches, and finished it on schedule. I then handed the headset back and haven't touched VR since. I also haven't stopped talking about it.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who already owns a headset and is looking for the killer app
  • Half-Life fans who accepted years ago that Half-Life 3 wasn't happening
  • People who like puzzles they can physically pick up and turn around

Dad caveats

  • You need a VR headset and a room to swing your arms in without hitting a bookshelf
  • Jeff will find you, and you will need to be very quiet
  • Motion sickness is real, and if you're prone to it, no amount of gravity glove is going to fix that

Receipts

One of us finished it on PC with an Index over a couple of weeks; the second played about half through a borrowed headset; the third watched twelve hours of VR footage and had opinions anyway. Details checked against the game's official Steam listing.
