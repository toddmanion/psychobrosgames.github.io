The thirty-second version

Factory building minus the ground and minus the panic.

Build a base in zero-G, misplace every wrench in three dimensions.

Exovia is the version of factory sims one of us has been asking for since Factorio: no fail state, no fires, no biters yelling at the fence. Just a chunk of asteroid and a slow, obsessive attempt to figure out how logistics work when there is no floor. It could be the best late-night decompression game of the show. If the camera controls are functional, we're in.

Okay, what is this thing?

Watch the trailer and the first thing you notice is that nothing is stacked. Machines float. Wrenches float. Presumably a dad in a spacesuit floats. The pitch is a factory sim built around procedurally generated asteroids in zero gravity, and the elevator description alone made two of us open a new tab. This is either the calmest sandbox game of the year or the point at which we admit we cannot think in three dimensions anymore.

The design deliberately strips out threat. There's no attack wave, no time bomb, no clock. Asteroid seeds are shareable, so the challenge is comparing solutions instead of racing them. Floating layouts replace conveyor belts, which sounds calm until you try to picture what an efficient one looks like without a floor. The whole thing lives or dies on the camera and the placement previews. If those work, we're building a base that has no business existing.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Factories are built in zero-G around procedurally generated asteroids, per the studio.
  • Asteroid seeds are shareable, so you can compare setups with another player.
  • There is no time pressure, no danger, and no fail state. On purpose.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Removing the fail state removes the reason I usually quit.

I've bounced off every factory game the other two have handed me, because I can't handle watching a base burn while I'm in the wrong menu. Take the threat away and suddenly I'm willing to spend an hour figuring out how ore gets from A to B. The one landmine is the 3D camera. If I have to fight it, I'm out before I've built the second machine.

02

The Descent Guy

Zero-G should add constraints, not just remove floor tiles.

A factory sim without a floor is either genuinely new or a chore with a fancy skybox. I want zero-G to change how throughput actually behaves. I also managed to invoke Descent within thirty seconds of the trailer, because of course six degrees of freedom got me. If the third dimension is where the puzzle actually lives, I'm writing an unnecessary essay by Sunday.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A calmer factory sim is a genuinely nice idea.

I can already see this replacing whatever I play when I can't handle another RPG boss. Shareable seeds mean I can send a screenshot to the other two and get judged, which is my favorite kind of accountability. My concern is milestones. Without a fail state, a factory sim needs visible reasons to keep playing. If the game has those, it's in my rotation. If not, it's a nice weekend.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Factory-sim fans who are tired of everything trying to kill them
  • Anyone who can genuinely visualize things in three dimensions (not us, but good for them)
  • Players who trade base screenshots the way other people trade recipes

Dad caveats

  • The 3D camera being a friend, not the boss fight
  • Meaningful goals when nothing is trying to burn down your base
  • Whether zero-G actually changes throughput or is scenery
