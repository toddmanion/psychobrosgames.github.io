The thirty-second version

Fifty games. A shocking number of hits.

It's like our childhood, if our childhood had also been proofread.

UFO 50 is the kind of thing you'd assume was a gimmick if the people behind it hadn't made Spelunky. It's fifty full games, most of them properly designed, several of them among the best small games of the year. It's an absurd amount of stuff for the money and a genuine argument about what the 8-bit era could have been.

Okay, what is this thing?

You boot the front-end, it looks like a piece of hardware you would have owned in 1984, and you double-click a game called Barbuta. Barbuta is hard. Barbuta is also great. Then you try one called Bushido Ball and it turns out you love volleyball with a katana. Six hours later you have opinions about eight of the fifty games and you have not left the sofa.

The real trick is how many of the fifty are actually good. There are shmups, RPGs, roguelikes, sports games, puzzle games, and a handful of things that don't fit any genre. They share a made-up publisher's history and a fake console spec sheet, and the shared limits give the collection a personality that a big themed anthology usually can't muster. It's a museum you can play.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • It's fifty complete games sharing a fictional 8-bit-era console and publisher history.
  • The games span genres from shmups to RPGs to sports to puzzle to real-time strategy.
  • It's from Mossmouth, the studio behind Spelunky, and it shows in the design bones.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I play exactly one of the fifty and refuse all the others.

I found Bushido Ball, decided it was my personality now, and I've played nothing else for weeks. I won't try Grimstone. I don't care about Mortol. When people ask me what UFO 50 is, I say 'volleyball, but Japanese'. I'm technically correct. The best kind of correct.

02

The Descent Guy

I wrote a ranking. Rewrote the ranking. Compared it to three online rankings.

I've got a full ranking with justifications. My top pick is a specific RPG we won't spoil, my bottom pick is a shmup I think unfairly reused its final boss, and my middle third is 'the ones nobody's talking about that are actually great'. I'm now writing a small essay about the collection's fake history. Nobody has tried to stop me and I'm choosing to read that as encouragement.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Even if half of them are duds, that's twenty-five good games.

I play it two or three games at a time and treat each session like a little sampler. Fair warning: the difficulty on a lot of them is genuinely 80s-hard, and a few will bounce anyone who isn't up for saving a lot. But at this price, for this many things, and this much design care, I can't think of a better deal we bought this year.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone with a fondness for the 8-bit era but not a lot of patience for the real thing
  • Group chats that need endless new game recommendations for the price of one
  • People who like an anthology with a story sitting quietly underneath it

Dad caveats

  • Some of the fifty are genuinely tough in the vintage way
  • You will play three, love one, and forget the others until later
  • The meta layer around the fake publisher is easy to miss and worth chasing
