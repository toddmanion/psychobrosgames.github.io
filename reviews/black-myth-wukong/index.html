The thirty-second version

Beautiful, brutal, occasionally overlong.

Finally caught up with what our children have been reading in books all this time.

Black Myth: Wukong is one of the most beautiful action games we've played and it's also more of a Souls than the marketing wanted to admit. The combat is heavy, the bosses are the whole show, and the mythology it draws on is a genuine delight. It's a bit long and a bit uneven in the middle, but the highs are stratospheric.

Okay, what is this thing?

You fight a wolf general, a giant spider, a bear the size of a temple, and every single one of them has a personal grievance with you. The Journey to the West source material gives Black Myth an art direction our library hasn't seen matched this year. Also, you can turn into different things. This never stops being funny.

Under the visuals is Souls-adjacent action combat that leans on staff stances, a stamina bar, and a handful of transformations and spells that recharge slowly. Bosses are where the game lives and dies — a couple are top-of-the-year, a couple over-repeat their patterns. The exploration is more linear than the trailers implied, but the boss-to-boss pace and the constant visual invention carry it further than that should.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • It's an action RPG drawn from the Chinese classic Journey to the West.
  • Combat runs on three staff stances, a stamina bar, and transformations that briefly turn you into other creatures.
  • Bosses are the whole spine of the game and there are a lot of them.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Souls-like difficulty has finally caught up with me.

I don't play Souls games. I gave this one a go on the strength of the trailers, made it to the first big wolf boss, died a lot, then respectfully closed the game. That wolf is my final boss, spiritually, and I'm happy to hear about the rest of the game from the other two at dinner. A perfectly reasonable Souls debut.

02

The Descent Guy

Staff stance timing is the whole meta and I've optimised it.

I tried all three staff stances, picked one, and never went back. My main note is that the middle chapters lean a bit hard on repeated enemy types and the level design gets a little corridor-shaped, but the boss encounters are worth the whole ticket. I'm still yelling about the tiger boss two months later, and nobody's worked out how to make me stop.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A long one, but the highs are worth the full sitting.

I finished it across about six weeks on PC and I think the pacing wobbles in the middle. I can also name four boss fights I'll remember for years, which is more than most action games this year gave me. If you don't already love Souls, this won't convert you, but if you're on the fence about mythology, come for the mythology.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Fans of Souls-likes who want a different mythology to fight through
  • People who like a game that runs on 'and then a bigger monkey appeared'
  • Anyone who wants to look at the prettiest bosses of the year

Dad caveats

  • The middle chapters can drag and repeat enemies
  • Difficulty spikes are a real thing and there's no easy mode
  • PC performance can wobble on older hardware

Receipts

Two of us finished it on PC, one bounced at the first major boss and hasn't returned. Facts double-checked against the game's official Steam listing.
