The thirty-second version

A chicken murder with actual casework underneath.

A chicken is dead. Three dads are already emotionally overinvested.

No Stone Unturned has a joke premise sitting on top of a genuinely structural detective game. Three linked cases from fairy tales, jokes, and children's stories all feed into A Murder Most Fowl, the chicken murder that gives the game its name. Ten-plus hours, fully voiced. The whole thing works if the humor sharpens observation instead of getting in the way of the actual deduction. If it does, this is a delight. If it doesn't, it's a comedy album with a magnifying glass.

Okay, what is this thing?

The premise is that a chicken has been murdered. The case, per the studio, is called A Murder Most Fowl. All three of us laughed exactly once at that name, immediately, out of self-respect. Underneath the pun is the actual game: three smaller cases inspired by fairy tales, jokes, and children's stories, each feeding into the central mystery. It is more detective than gag, which is a genuinely good sign for a comedy game.

The pitch is more than ten hours of story with a fully voice-acted cast. British humor is the tone. What we can't tell from the trailer is whether the deduction actually works: whether a stalled case has a hint system that helps, whether a solved case feels earned, whether the humor is doing the heavy lifting or the mystery is. The good version is that the jokes make you pay closer attention. The bad version is that every clue is a setup for a punchline.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Comedy softens the pressure of being wrong.

I'm genuinely bad at adventure games. I solve nothing. What keeps me playing is when the game's funny about it. A voiced cast making a joke about my terrible theory is a much better failure state than a wrong-answer chime. My one worry is a case stalling. If I'm stuck for twenty minutes without a nudge, I close the game and forget it exists.

02

The Descent Guy

A funny game still needs consistent evidence.

I'm fine with a comedy tone. What I refuse to accept is a whodunnit that hides the answer behind arbitrary logic and then punches me for being reasonable. I want evidence that adds up, suspects who behave like people, and a solution that'd still make sense with the jokes removed. If the game clears that bar, the humor is a bonus. If not, no amount of British charm will save it.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A ten-hour voiced adventure is exactly a Saturday night.

Detective games with real voice acting are my favorite couch pick, because someone can sit next to me and yell theories. I can already picture the spouse-and-dad accusation sessions over the chicken murder. Ten hours is a runtime I can plan around. My concern is comedy repetition. If the same three joke shapes carry the whole thing, hour eight feels tired.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Adventure fans who want their comedy voiced, not just written
  • Couples and small groups who love yelling theories at each other
  • Anyone who unironically enjoys British humor and storybook parody

Dad caveats

  • How generous the hint system is when a case stalls
  • Whether solutions feel deduced, not delivered
  • Comedy staying fresh at hour eight of ten
