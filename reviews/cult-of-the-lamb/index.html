The thirty-second version

Charming. Also a spreadsheet in fleece.

My cult expanded rapidly once I introduced dental.

Cult of the Lamb marries a roguelite dungeon-crawler to a village management sim. The village half is great, the dungeon half is fine, and the doctrine of 'these two loops feed each other' is what carries it. It's a game about cleaning up after true believers who won't stop pooping in the church. Which is a review in itself.

Okay, what is this thing?

You are a lamb. You die. A giant demon god called The One Who Waits offers you a bargain: he'll keep you alive, you have to build a cult in his name. Two hours later you're managing bathroom schedules for six wolves, a lizard, and a very disappointed frog. The art is Cuphead-cute, the tone is Adventure Time meets Wickerman, and the game is entirely aware of how funny that is.

The core loop is dungeon runs to gather resources and followers, then base management back at the compound to feed, sleep and radicalise them. The dungeon side is a serviceable roguelite; the base side is where the game lives, with rituals, buildings, and the fastest slide from wholesome to unholy in recent memory. If you love cosies with a mean streak, this is calibrated for you.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • A hybrid of a roguelite dungeon-crawler and a base-building village sim.
  • Followers age, get sick, and require your attention or leave the flock.
  • Doctrine choices unlock unique rituals and, honestly, a lot of the humour.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I wanted farming and I got farming with a knife.

I was in it for the animals and the cottagecore. I was less in it for the third combat run of the day. I got about eight hours in, at which point my cult had five levels of tents, and I stopped. The village sim is very good. I just wish there was more of it and fewer knives.

02

The Descent Guy

This is what a good hybrid looks like, and I'm now taking questions.

I mapped the dungeon-to-village conversion rates on a napkin. I worked out which follower traits stack for which rituals. I beat the second bishop with an axe build and then made a spreadsheet about it. My only complaint is that the roguelite side has fewer weapon families than I'd like, which is a compliment. The rest of the design is exactly my thing.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Fifteen hours, four bosses, one cult, job done.

I finished the main story arc in about fifteen hours and stopped. The updates since launch have added stuff, but I have a life. As a one-and-done it's charming, it has a proper ending, and it never overstays. I also have a follower named after my real dog, and I won't let anyone excommunicate him.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Fans of Stardew who want a mean streak in their cosy
  • People with a favourite pet name they can put on a sheep
  • Anyone who likes cult humour and doesn't take it too seriously

Dad caveats

  • The dungeon half is fine, but you'll notice it isn't the point
  • Late-game village management can lean heavy on chores
  • Your followers will die and you will be more upset than you expected

Receipts

Two of us reached the main ending on PC in roughly fifteen hours each; the third stayed at the village and never went back to the dungeons.
