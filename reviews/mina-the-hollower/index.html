The thirty-second version

Yacht Club still know exactly what they're doing.

The main character has a whip. That's the pitch. That's a great pitch.

Mina the Hollower is a beautifully cranky little action-adventure that plays like a Game Boy Color cartridge you never actually owned but somehow miss. The whip feels great, the burrow is a real movement idea, and the whole thing runs on the same taste and craft that made Shovel Knight so easy to love. If you've got a nice controller, use it here.

Okay, what is this thing?

You play Mina, a Hollower, which is apparently the medieval term for 'a small hero with a whip and a bad attitude.' She's dropped onto a cursed island and told to fix it, and inside twenty minutes you've cracked something small and rude with the whip and burrowed under a wall to find a secret. Yacht Club Games have made a game that feels like the childhood cartridge you told everyone else about.

The whip and the burrow are the whole thing, and they're enough. Combat is precise and readable, the burrow lets you cheat around encounters in ways that feel earned, and the environments give you real reasons to look for hidden holes to dive into. It's tightly designed in the way indie throwbacks either nail or completely botch, and Yacht Club nailed it.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • You play Mina, a Hollower, with a whip primary and a burrow move that reshapes traversal.
  • From Yacht Club Games, the studio behind Shovel Knight, and it shows in every pixel.
  • Presented in a hand-crafted pixel style that reads like a Game Boy Color game with modern sensibilities.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The whip felt good instantly, which is the entire fight.

I don't have time to learn combos. Mina has a whip, and a whip is a one-button verb, which is my exact speed. I got through the first area with almost no menu screens opened, which coming from me is a five-star review. I also love the burrow, because it lets me skip fights I don't fancy.

02

The Descent Guy

A movement toolkit with layers I can't stop mapping.

I was three rooms in before I started asking whether the burrow has i-frames. It might. It might not. I'm testing. I love that a small pixel game has a real toolkit under the surface, and I'm building routes through screens I'm fairly sure the designers didn't intend. I'm probably wrong about that. I don't care.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A great post-dinner game if you don't fancy an epic.

I get an hour after dinner and this fills it perfectly. Bite-size encounters, a save that never punishes me for stepping away, and enough taste in the art that even a bad night still looks lovely. Absolute palate cleanser between the longer games I'm supposed to be finishing and am not.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone with a soft spot for Game Boy Color pixel work
  • Shovel Knight loyalists who want the same taste in a new shape
  • Dads with a controller drawer and no time for 80-hour RPGs

Dad caveats

  • It is proudly retro, which for some folks reads as 'small' rather than 'tight'
  • The burrow is a real skill you have to actually learn; button-mashing hurts
  • You will find yourself humming the theme in the shower like an idiot

Receipts

Played on PC by two of us with a controller across a couple of weeks, mostly after bedtime. The third watched a stream and immediately bought it.
