The thirty-second version

It's a hangout. It rules.

Finally, a co-op game where nobody has a health bar to argue about.

Big Walk is a small, warm, extremely House House co-op game about wandering around a big world with your friends and getting lost on purpose. There's no threat, no boss, just a walk and a chat and enough oddball stuff to point at that we happily lost three nights to it. It's a lovely time.

Okay, what is this thing?

We booted up Big Walk on a Wednesday, all three of us in voice chat, and inside two minutes one of us had wandered off to look at a rock and the other two had lost him entirely. That was the game. That was the whole game. House House have made something that feels like the digital equivalent of taking a walk with your mates, which is a rare feeling in your forties.

There is no combat and there are no fail states, and the point of the game is walking around a shared world together and being surprised by the little things in it. The design assumes your voice chat is doing at least half the work, and it's right. It's the sort of game we didn't know we wanted until we were sitting in it, saying 'oh, look at that,' for two hours straight.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Cooperative online walker-talker, from the studio behind Untitled Goose Game.
  • No combat, no fail states; the game is the walk and the conversation.
  • Published by Panic, which is a mark of good taste on its own.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

A co-op game I can play without ever opening a menu.

I walked, I talked, I pointed at a bird, I was happy. There's nothing here that needs me to remember a control scheme. I've suggested Big Walk on three separate group-chat nights now and nobody has said no once. I'm doing my best work on this one.

02

The Descent Guy

I turned my brain off and it was medicine.

I came in expecting to find hidden systems and found a peaceful world built for aimless wandering instead. I complained for eleven minutes and then quietly enjoyed myself for two hours. There's a real lesson in that. The other two aren't qualified to teach it. I'm not qualified to receive it. I've already asked when we're playing again.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The best 'while we catch up' game we've played in years.

This is what I load when the group chat wants to hang out but nobody wants to concentrate. Voice chat's on, tabs are open, the jokes are terrible. I rate it highly on the specific metric of 'still fun while you also talk about your kids for forty minutes.' Which is a metric almost no games score on.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Group chats that want to hang out without committing to a raid
  • House House fans who wanted more of the same weird warmth
  • Anyone who needs a co-op game that respects a bedtime

Dad caveats

  • There genuinely isn't a challenge; if you need a scoreboard, look elsewhere
  • It leans hard on you actually being on voice chat with people you like
  • Solo works but it's clearly built for a group; herd friends before booting

Receipts

Played in three-player online sessions on PC across a few evenings, headsets on, kids in bed, and one of us occasionally getting up to check on a suspicious noise upstairs.
