The thirty-second version

Best call from the group chat, every time.

The last time three of us wore matching hats we were on holiday and lost.

Deep Rock Galactic is the game none of us has ever uninstalled. It runs coop missions that fit inside a weeknight, has four classes that need each other in ways most coop games only claim, and it has never once made any of us feel stupid for being tired. There's a keg on the Space Rig. There's a barrel-hoop mini-game. There's genuine friendship happening down there.

Okay, what is this thing?

The Space Rig is a small orbital canteen with a working bar, a jukebox, a promotion terminal, and, for reasons only Ghost Ship know, a hoop you can toss beer barrels into. This is where every mission begins and ends. Then you drop into a hole full of glowing rocks, alien bugs and one mineral you specifically forgot to mine, and forty minutes later you come back to that same bar and buy each other virtual drinks.

The mission types are what keeps it fresh — Salvage, Egg Hunt, Point Extraction, Escort Duty, On-Site Refining. Every one demands a slightly different plan and rewards a slightly different loadout. Combine that with four asymmetric classes — Driller, Engineer, Gunner, Scout — and every session ends up being a small, specific improvisation instead of a template. It's the coop equivalent of an old friendly pub. Every time you show up, it works.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • There are half a dozen mission types, each with its own tactical shape.
  • Seasonal content has been free since launch, which we do not take for granted.
  • Yes, there's a barrel-hoop mini-game on the Space Rig, and it is the most important part of the entire product.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The class that lets me think for a second.

I picked Engineer on my first mission because turrets sounded easier than aiming, and I've never played anything else. I drop platforms on demand, I set up my sentries, and then I mine while the other two handle the swarm. The game counts that as helping — it is helping, actually — and I get to finish the mission feeling like I did my part. Which is genuinely rare in a shooter.

02

The Descent Guy

The four classes are four verbs and every one is load-bearing.

I've got opinions on optimal loadouts for Elite Deep Dives, arranged in my notes app. I can tell you which secondary is best for a Salvage mission at Hazard 5. I also insist Scout is a support class, which starts an argument every single evening, and I am, technically, correct. I usually play Driller because I like drawing walls.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The game that has outlasted every other coop hit we've bought.

I play maybe once a week, maybe once a fortnight, and it never feels rusty. There's no rank pressure, no season pass to keep up with, no menu shame when I log in after a month off. Missions are thirty to forty minutes, matchmaking finds you people who yell nice things at you, and you can drop out mid-mission if a kid appears. That's why it's still on my hard drive.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Groups whose skill levels differ by 'wildly'
  • Anyone whose evenings end without notice
  • People who miss playing coop games with actual roles

Dad caveats

  • The early hours drop resources more slowly than the late game does
  • You will develop opinions about grunts, and they will affect your life
  • Playing solo is possible but you'll be doing it with a drone, and the drone does not tell jokes back
