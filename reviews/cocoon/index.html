The thirty-second version

Every puzzle earns its little 'oh'.

Nested puzzles, nested naps. We finished them both.

COCOON is the kind of puzzle game where you spend forty minutes stuck, one thing clicks, and the whole level rearranges itself in your head like a magic trick. It's short, dialogue-free, and every mechanic makes you feel like a genius when you crack it. Easy recommendation for anyone who liked LIMBO or INSIDE and wants the third one in that DNA.

Okay, what is this thing?

You are a bug. You walk up to an orb the size of your bug body and you pick it up and, inside the orb, there is a whole world. You walk that world into another orb. That's the whole hook. Two hours in it starts stacking them and you can feel your brain making sounds it hasn't made since university.

The rest is genuinely clever level design and lovely, muted art direction. There are boss fights, of a sort — patterned, puzzle-shaped ones — and the game teaches every mechanic wordlessly so you never once feel talked down to. It runs about six hours, which is exactly the right length. Every puzzle earns its 'oh' and none of them wear out their welcome.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • It's from Jeppe Carlsen, the lead gameplay designer on LIMBO and INSIDE, and it feels like it.
  • There is no dialogue, no HUD, and no on-screen prompts, and it still explains itself perfectly.
  • You can beat it in a weekend and think about it for a month.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I never once opened a menu, because there aren't any.

This is my ideal setup. No text. No tooltips. You just walk somewhere and something happens. I got through the first two worlds in a sitting, got stuck for an hour, refused help, and eventually solved it out loud while making dinner. It's the first puzzle game I've finished since Portal, and I wouldn't stop telling the other two about it.

02

The Descent Guy

The topology is the puzzle. I'm in heaven.

I drew a diagram. A real one, on real paper. I'm currently trying to articulate whether the fourth orb is a subset or a peer of the third and it's making me happier than any game this year. My only note is that a couple of puzzles are more parse-the-visual than think-the-thought, but I forgave it. Because it's beautiful.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Six hours, no filler, no season pass. What a relief.

I played it over three evenings and then spent a fourth one talking about it. It sits perfectly in the slot where you want something that isn't a live service and isn't seventy hours long. My only real caveat is that the boss fights, while charming, are the weakest bits, and you'll want to save mid-puzzle just in case a phone call happens.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • LIMBO and INSIDE fans who trust the whole team
  • Anyone who wants a puzzle game that respects a busy month
  • People who like to be a bug

Dad caveats

  • There's no hint system, and you will be stuck for real at some point
  • The boss fights are the least strong part, though still fine
  • It ends. You'll wish it didn't.

Receipts

Two of us cleared it on PC across a weekend, the third took about a week between commutes. Facts double-checked against the game's official Steam page.
