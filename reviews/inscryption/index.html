The thirty-second version

You can only play it once. Play it once.

First deckbuilder where the deckbuilder wants to build you into the deck.

Inscryption is a card game about being in a cabin with a horror. Then it isn't. Then it isn't again. Any specific description is a spoiler. What we can say is that it's three distinct card games in one story, at least two of them are genuinely great, and the puzzling around the table is as good as the puzzling on it. Go in blind.

Okay, what is this thing?

You sit down at a table. The bloke across from you does not have a face. There is a stoat, and the stoat has notes. You play a couple of hands and lose, and then someone leaves a scalpel on the desk and suggests you might want to have a look around the cabin. This is roughly the point at which we started sending each other cryptic voice notes at 1am.

Mechanically it's a card game where every creature costs blood, and blood costs cards you already had on the board. That single decision structure ripples through everything: sacrifice, planning, which of your squirrels you love most. Then the game changes, and the mechanics change with it, and to say any more is to ruin the trick. It's a magic show. Do not read the reviews. Play the thing.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • A card game whose primary resource is sacrificing the creatures you already play.
  • Three distinct acts, each with a meaningfully different card system.
  • Meta-plot content unfolds via ARG-style discoveries during the game.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The cabin ruined a Wednesday and I thank it.

I don't usually play card games. I finished the first act because the horror man is charismatic. I don't know why the game keeps talking about a video store and I don't want to be told. I said 'is my computer okay?' out loud at one point, which is the highest compliment I can pay a work of art.

02

The Descent Guy

One resource, one board, and I cracked it open like a walnut.

The sacrifice loop had me from turn three. I'd worked out that stoats stack, that hoarders enable combos, that Leshy is looking at your hand and reading you back. I haven't been this happy with a card game economy since I got mad about Slay the Spire's Watcher. I'd like a comfier chair, but that one's on me.

03

Normal-ish Dad

You play it once and that's the deal.

About twelve hours, one long weekend. The story is the point, and the story doesn't rerun. I'm not doing NG+. I'm not touching Kaycee's Mod for months. I've told everyone in the group chat to buy it and then go silent, and that's a review in itself.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who liked Slay the Spire but wants to be haunted by it
  • People who go into things blind and love it
  • Fans of Pony Island who've been asking Daniel Mullins for a big one

Dad caveats

  • Act two is the divisive one. He may or may not vibe with it
  • Do not, under any circumstances, watch a trailer
  • You can only play it once. Buy accordingly

Receipts

All three of us played it to credits on PC across roughly a fortnight; discussions were held in a chat we now refuse to open.
