The thirty-second version

Style ranks. That's the pitch.

The last time we got an SSS rank at anything, our kids were being born.

Devil May Cry 5 is Capcom at maximum swagger, and every one of us grinned at least once per mission. It gives you three completely different characters and a scoring system that grades your combat like a strict but fair PE teacher. It's not deep, exactly, but it's dense — every button does a thing, and the thing is cool.

Okay, what is this thing?

You are Nero. You have a robot arm called a Devil Breaker. It has punched things, then exploded, and then you have laughed. Then the game hands you V, a goth with a cane who summons a giant bird, a panther and a golem while reciting poetry. Then it hands you Dante and you realise you had no idea what button-mashing could look like.

The core loop is fights, style meter, next fight, style meter. Every character has a completely different toolkit — Nero's arms are consumable and swappable, V's animals do the work while he reads a book, and Dante has four styles and eight weapons and no discernible cooldown between any of them. Missions run twenty minutes or so, and the game rewards replaying them the way an old arcade cabinet did.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Three playable characters, all with completely different combat systems.
  • Style ranks go from D up to SSS, and yes, they are addictive.
  • Runs on the RE Engine, which is why every strand of Dante's hair moves independently.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The rare action game where mashing looks fine.

I played Nero. I picked whichever Devil Breaker was on the shelf. I got C ranks throughout and considered that a personal best. The important thing is that Nero looked incredibly cool during those C ranks, because DMC5 is built so even beginner-tier combos are big splashy things, and I genuinely enjoyed a genre I normally avoid on principle.

02

The Descent Guy

Three protagonists, three fully realised combat games in one box.

I immediately locked in on V. V does nothing directly — his familiars do the damage while he trots around the arena reading. It's, mechanically, position management with delayed inputs, and it's the most Descent-adjacent thing in a game about a demon in leather trousers. I then went back and rank-graded every mission on Dante, because of course I did.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The best way to remember why you loved action games.

Missions are short. Twenty, twenty-five minutes. Cutscenes cut. I can pick it up for an hour, hit two demons very hard and put it down feeling like a whole person. I didn't finish it in a straight line, and it didn't punish me for taking two months off between mission sixteen and mission seventeen.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • People who used to feed pounds into Sega cabinets
  • Anyone who wants a AAA game they can finish in bite-sized pieces
  • Dads who need to remember what fun looks like at high volume

Dad caveats

  • The story is nonsense in a way you will need to accept early
  • V is not everyone's cup of tea and that is fine
  • You will absolutely try to look up how to reach SSS rank and then feel old

Receipts

Two of us finished the campaign across a couple of months on PC; the third loved the Nero missions and drifted before Dante fully unlocked. Details checked against the game's official Steam listing.
