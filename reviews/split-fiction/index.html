The thirty-second version

Hazelight did it again.

Turns out the best fantasy setting is 'someone else has to be player two.'

Split Fiction is a co-op adventure built to be played with exactly one other person on the couch, which is a heroic assumption for our lives, but boy is it worth clearing an evening for. It swaps genres every twenty minutes and never quite runs out of ideas. If you have someone to play it with, cancel your plans.

Okay, what is this thing?

We spent our first session with two of us on the couch, one dad hovering behind us with a beer, and inside forty minutes we'd been dragons, gunships, and something we couldn't describe out loud without laughing. Hazelight makes co-op games that assume you and your partner enjoy each other's company, which is either lovely or a red flag depending on the marriage.

The whole thing is built on constant reinvention. Each set-piece hands you two different tools and asks the two of you to solve one problem, and the answers get sillier as they get more ambitious. You can play remotely, but the game is engineered for a shared sofa and a shared groan when one of you drops the wrong glowing thing off the wrong glowing ledge.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Two-player only, and every set-piece is designed around a shared couch or a shared voice call.
  • Chops between wildly different sci-fi and fantasy scenarios, and doesn't reuse a mechanic if it can invent a new one.
  • From the studio that made It Takes Two, so you already know what your kids will find you doing at 10pm.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I was the second player and I stayed the second player.

I normally bail at the first tutorial pop-up. I got through Split Fiction because it never once asked me to remember anything from twenty minutes ago. Whatever tool the game handed me this room, that was the tool for this room. I also refused to swap seats even once, which the other two say is how you can tell I liked it.

02

The Descent Guy

A co-op game whose puzzles actually need two brains.

I was ready to be annoyed and instead caught myself running spreadsheets for a physics puzzle that lasted five minutes. Every scene asks the two of you to solve a slightly different problem, and I love being useful. The gag is that my optimal solution and their chaotic one usually meet halfway with laughs, and I'm slowly learning to accept that outcome.

03

Normal-ish Dad

It's a whole weekend of couch time if you can carve one out.

A meaty co-op that respects your marriage and your bedtime, in chapters you can bail out of cleanly. I'd rather do this with a real person than any online lobby, and it's the first game in a while all three of us actually cleared. Bring snacks. Bring patience for at least one 'you go left, no MY left.'

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone with one specific person they want to play games with
  • Couples who can survive the phrase 'no, YOUR left'
  • People who miss the era when a game just kept throwing new ideas at you

Dad caveats

  • It really is two players only, so if your co-op partner cancels you're stuck
  • The tonal whiplash between genres is the whole point, but it is whiplash
  • You will lose an evening to a mini-game that has no business being that good

Receipts

Played to completion by two of us on the couch across three long evenings, with the third eating chips and directing traffic like a very tall referee.
