The thirty-second version

It's a speedrun game about being late to heaven.

First shooter that made a middle-aged man voluntarily replay a level. Terrifying.

Neon White is a first-person speedrunning platformer whose central idea is genuinely new: your gun is a card, and you can either shoot with it or discard it for a movement ability. Never both. Every level is a small puzzle about which of your six cards to fire and which to throw away. It is criminally addictive and also has a lot of anime dialogue you'll have to accept.

Okay, what is this thing?

Heaven has a demon problem. They've drafted an assassin called White to solve it, and he isn't hanging about. Every gun he picks up doubles as a movement move: the pistol becomes a double jump, the shotgun becomes a dash, the rifle becomes a soar. The joke is that you always have to pick: kill this demon, or use the gun to reach the next platform. Never both. The devils know.

The mechanic is elegant and the level design is exceptional. Every level is a puzzle you'll solve in a minute and chase for another thirty. There's a friends-times leaderboard that will absolutely eat your evening. The dialogue between the levels is bright, loud, and made for a younger crowd. You skip through it. The levels are the point.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • A first-person speedrun platformer where guns double as one-shot movement cards.
  • Each level is 30-90 seconds long, with gold and ace times to chase.
  • Friend leaderboards let you compare specific times per stage against people you know.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

'Try to beat the level' turned into 'I'm going to bed' quickly.

I liked the movement. I liked jumping about. What I didn't want was to redo the same level for the eighth time to shave 1.4 seconds off it. I got the first world's golds and dismounted. If this were called Neon Chill and had no timer I'd still be playing. As it is, it's not for me.

02

The Descent Guy

Cards as a resource and movement as a puzzle, and I'm in heaven.

I'd been asking for a game like this and refusing to believe it existed. Every stage is a hand management puzzle wearing a first-person shooter. Which gun do I fire, which do I throw away, which do I save for the last jump? I've got aces on the first world now, and strong opinions about mouse versus controller for it.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Ninety-second levels are what our lives look like.

The pitch is that any level is under two minutes. I can play three between putting the kids down and starting the laundry. The obvious weakness, the anime writing, is skippable. The obvious strength, how it feels to nail a run, is not. I won't finish it. I'll enjoy every session I do play.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Speedrun brains who want the game to hand them the puzzle already
  • Anyone whose gaming windows are literally 90 seconds long
  • People who watch AGDQ and have opinions about IL rankings

Dad caveats

  • The dialogue is very much an acquired taste
  • On mouse and keyboard it feels perfect. On controller it feels almost as good
  • You will replay levels until 1am. This is not a suggestion

Receipts

Two of us cleared roughly a third of the main campaign across a fortnight on PC; the third got to the second world and switched to ace-hunting.
