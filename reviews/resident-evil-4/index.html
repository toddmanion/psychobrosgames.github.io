The thirty-second version

Still the greatest bad first date.

Turns out marriage counselling was Leon rescuing Ashley the whole time.

The Resident Evil 4 remake takes a game two of us already thought was untouchable and finds a way to make it better. Leon controls like a proper action hero, the parry adds a whole new rhythm, and the village opening is still the best hour in survival horror. If you didn't play the original, this is the version to start with. If you did, this is somehow still worth it.

Okay, what is this thing?

There is a moment early on where a mob of villagers surrounds a farmhouse, one of them is holding a chainsaw, and Leon calmly rolls under a table. Two of us have played that sequence a dozen times over twenty years and we still had to pause and breathe. The remake completely nails the pacing of the village while giving it a texture the original couldn't afford.

The parry is the big new toy and it changes everything. Suddenly the knife isn't just a knife, it's a get-out-of-Ganado free card, and Leon feels dangerous in a way he didn't in 2005. The merchant is back, Ashley is a real person this time, and the shooting galleries are still the funniest side content in the series. It's the rare remake that respects the original enough to actually change things.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • The village is set in an unnamed part of rural Spain and it is genuinely, correctly frightening.
  • The parry works on almost everything including axes, pitchforks and the occasional bit of horror-movie physics.
  • Ashley is no longer a health bar with legs and it improves every scene she's in.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The stress of the village broke me. I came back for the castle.

I died five times at the farmhouse fight and put the game down for a fortnight. Then curiosity got me. Once the shotgun showed up I stopped panicking and started planning, and by the end I was calmly popping legs and reloading like a man who watches gun content on YouTube. Which I now do, apparently.

02

The Descent Guy

Optimal upgrade routes, aggressive parries, zero pity for Ashley.

I treated the whole game as an inventory-management puzzle and had spare grenades by Chapter 4. My actual thesis is that parrying a chainsaw is the closest thing modern shooters have to a real skill check, and the other two tell me watching me do it back-to-back at Professional difficulty was educational. I also refuse to sell the handcannon.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A twenty-hour action game that never stops finding new toys.

The pacing is the actual miracle. Village, castle, island — every act has a new hook and it never once felt like padding. I beat it across about three weeks of school-night sessions and every session ended at a satisfying stopping point. If more AAA games understood that we sometimes have to close the game at nine forty-five, we'd finish more of them.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who missed the original because they were busy having children in 2005
  • People who want a horror game with more shotguns than jump scares
  • Fans of the merchant, and let's be honest, that's everyone

Dad caveats

  • The village opening is genuinely stressful and does not ease you in
  • Ashley sections still exist and one of them is a lot
  • You will start doing the merchant's laugh in real life

Receipts

All three of us finished it on PC on Standard, one of us went back for a Hardcore run. Details verified against the game's official Steam listing.
