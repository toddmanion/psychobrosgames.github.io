The thirty-second version

The best local council election in RPGs.

Every party meeting ends in arson. So does every real one, honestly.

Divinity: Original Sin 2 is the best four-player RPG we have ever run at the same table. Turn-based combat that reads the room, party members who can straight-up disagree with your dialogue choices, and a Fort Joy opening so dense you'll reroll a character just to see what you missed. It is a hundred-hour commitment. It is completely worth it.

Okay, what is this thing?

The first tutorial we all remember is one of us casting a rain spell on a corpse fire, which cooled the fire but flooded the corridor, which the enemy mage then electrified, which killed three of us and half of a merchant. That is how we learned about elemental combos. That is also how we learned who was going to be a problem for the whole campaign.

The turn-based combat is a physics puzzle. Ground surfaces stack: oil becomes fire becomes smoke becomes cursed fire, and every ability is a small lever you can push on that stack. Around it Larian have built a properly reactive world, with quest solutions that assume you'll try dumb things. Four-player co-op means the party can split, argue with itself, and vote against you in dialogue. Nothing has felt more like a real tabletop campaign.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Fully cooperative for up to four players, each with their own character sheet and dialogue choices.
  • Combat surfaces persist and interact: oil, fire, water, electricity, poison, blood, cursed variants of all of the above, all stacking.
  • Origin characters have their own quests, so replaying with a different lead genuinely reshuffles the game.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I'd never touch this solo. In our party, I was gold.

I'd never touch this alone. In our four-player run I played a rogue, stole everything not nailed down, and once slept-arrowed an entire boss fight while the others did the actual work. I had a great time. So did they. I still couldn't tell you what Source is, and honestly, at this point, neither could a lot of players.

02

The Descent Guy

Necrofire is not just fire, apparently.

I turned the loading screens into lectures. I built a summoner-necromancer who kept undead armies on the field for eight-turn fights, and I laughed in a way that made the other two worry. This is the game that finally beat Descent as my default conversation-starter, which is a real sentence about a real man, and that man is me.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Get four dads on a weekly slot and it repays you tenfold.

Getting four dads into one campaign is like herding fireflies. We managed it for about six months and finished Act 2. I think that's a legitimate victory. The pacing rewards long sessions and yes it does drag in the third act, but the highs are the highest co-op RPG has produced for us since the previous Larian game.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Groups of three or four who can lock in a weekly session
  • Players who miss Baldur's Gate 3 already and want to know where it came from
  • Anyone who has ever wanted to be the party's designated problem

Dad caveats

  • Act 3 has known pacing issues that even the Definitive Edition did not fully solve
  • Character build regret is real and respec-ing is doable but a chore
  • One player will absolutely become the arsonist, and it will always be the same person

Receipts

Weekly four-player co-op for around six months on PC, plus a solo run to see the origin content the campaign group missed.
