Resident Evil Village

Nine-foot vampire lady got the marketing, but this is really a fairy tale horror anthology in a coat.

The thirty-second version

Tall lady, short opinions, big value.

Finally an RE where the neck aches from looking up, not down at ammo counts.

Village is a fine sequel with four dramatically different setpieces stapled together by an Eastern European hamlet. When it's a haunted castle, it's brilliant. When it's a doll house, it's the best hour of horror we've had since Amnesia. When it's a factory, it's a factory. Worth every hour anyway.

Okay, what is this thing?

Ethan Winters gets his hands mangled again. There is a village. There is a lady who is nine feet tall and has become the entire personality of an internet. All three of us played it in first person on separate PCs and all three of us jumped in exactly the same place, which we now discuss on group chat like it's a shared trauma.

Structurally, it's four horror games with a shared spine. Castle Dimitrescu is a hide-and-seek mansion with a very tall stalker. House Beneviento is a psychological detour that would frighten a corpse. Moreau is a wet mini-boss run. Heisenberg's factory is a shooter. The variety is the pitch and also the wobble; you'll love at least two of them, and put up with one.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • First-person survival horror across four distinct 'lords' with different tones.
  • Runs on the RE Engine and is remarkably well-optimised on modest PCs.
  • Includes The Mercenaries arcade mode alongside the main campaign.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I loved the castle. I did not love the doll house.

Halfway through Beneviento I took my headphones off, said 'no thank you', and didn't touch it again for a month. When I came back I was fine, and I finished it with the sound down. I'd like the record to show that the scariest thing I've ever heard in a video game came out of that mansion, and I want no medal for admitting it.

02

The Descent Guy

The economy is generous, and I took that as a challenge.

I immediately started running knife-only wherever I could. I kept a mental tally of Lei per encounter. I'm convinced the Duke's shop bends generosity into a puzzle you can pick apart. I'm right about that. I'm told I'm less right about the Heisenberg wave-defence bit, which I insist is a Descent room. Apparently it is not a Descent room.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Twelve hours, four flavours, and a proper credits roll.

I knocked it out over a long weekend and had no notes on pacing. The variety kept me awake, the runtime respected my back, and no chapter overstayed its welcome. I'd trade the factory for another Beneviento in a heartbeat, though I do understand why they can't make an entire game out of the Beneviento.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Fans of RE4 who want more of what makes RE4 work
  • Anyone in the mood for genuinely varied horror across one weekend
  • People who wish their in-laws were nine feet tall and Romanian

Dad caveats

  • House Beneviento is not messing about, and you will know it when it starts
  • The story leans on RE7 lore in ways that a first-time player will have to google
  • Heisenberg's factory is the weakest hour, and it's a long hour

Receipts

Two of us finished the main campaign on PC across roughly twelve hours each; the third bailed after Beneviento and came back a month later.
