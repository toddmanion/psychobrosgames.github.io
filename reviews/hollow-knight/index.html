The thirty-second version

Bugs, sad piano, and a map that fights back.

We came for the bugs. Stayed for the emotional damage.

Hollow Knight is the game we all keep restarting, because nobody wants to admit they forgot where the second dream nail upgrade was. It's brutal in a very quiet way, gorgeous in a way that outlasted its screenshots, and the sort of thing you either sink ninety hours into or bounce off in the first cavern. All three happened here.

Okay, what is this thing?

You wake up in a windswept town called Dirtmouth, next to an old bug who looks like he has already given up on you. Then you drop into a hole. Somewhere below there's a kingdom of dead insects, a corrupted plague, and the exact same jumping puzzle three times in a row. Somehow this is charming. Somehow we all keep going back.

The genius is that you don't get a map from a menu. You find a mole cartographer sitting in the middle of a cave, and you buy quills off him. So the map lags one biome behind you, forever. Combat is nail-and-spell, dodging is a dash, healing takes a full second while a giant maggot chews on your ankle. It reads simple. It is not.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Hallownest is one interconnected hand-drawn map, no random generation, all built by two people plus a small team.
  • Healing costs the same soul you use for spells, so every fight is a tiny budget crisis.
  • There's a fast-travel system, but you have to walk to every stagbeetle station first. Cruel.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I'm stuck at False Knight, forever.

I got as far as False Knight, died six times, and put it down for a year. Every time the other two mention it I say 'the one with the sad bug', nod, and start a different game. I'm not wrong to bounce. There's no assist mode, no difficulty toggle, and the map thing is a real ask on a work night.

02

The Descent Guy

Charm loadouts have entered the group chat.

I rebound every button, kept a text file of charm loadouts, and had opinions about shaman stone versus quick slash three hours in. I also finished it. Twice. The nail combat scratches my precision itch, and the wandering map system rewards the sort of brain that draws its own subway maps on napkins.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Fifty hours, done in twenty-minute slices.

I finished the main path, saw one of the endings, and quietly closed the game before Path of Pain existed to me. Correct move for a dad. Fifty hours is a huge ask, but the sessions bank well. You can play forty minutes, remember exactly where you are next time, and slot right back in. That's rare.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who owns a comfortable controller and a working sense of grievance
  • People who like drawing their own maps in their heads
  • Solo evenings when the kids are finally asleep and you want to feel something

Dad caveats

  • No assist options and no difficulty toggle at all
  • The map system is genuinely part of the puzzle, which will annoy some of you
  • Charm synergy discussions will absolutely leak into your real conversations

Receipts

Two of us finished the main path on PC over roughly seventy hours combined; the third put in about four hours before politely returning to Stardew Valley.
