The thirty-second version

Hitting lizards is fine, actually.

I now own a spreadsheet called Great Jagras Farming Route. Please send help.

Monster Hunter: World is the entry we tell people to start with, because it finally learned how to explain itself. The hunts are cinematic, the fourteen weapons play like fourteen different games, and the four-player co-op works even when one of us has to leave at exactly 9:47. Some of the menus still assume you've played six of these before. That's the tax.

Okay, what is this thing?

You are in a forest. A dinosaur the size of a hotel is standing in your way, and one of your friends has a horn instead of a sword. You spend twenty minutes learning its patterns, break its tail off with a great sword, and drag it back to base camp so a chef can turn it into steaks. This is the entire game, and it is somehow one of the best things our group has done together in a decade.

Fourteen weapons that play like fourteen different games, hunts that run twenty to forty minutes, and monsters that feel less like enemies and more like weather systems. There's a real ecology to it: apex predators knock lesser ones out of biomes, elder dragons rearrange terrain, and every big fight has phases you learn like a boxing match. It rewards a small commitment to reading its systems, and it punishes just about everything else.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Fourteen weapon types, all mechanically distinct. Great sword, insect glaive, and hunting horn share basically nothing.
  • Multiplayer supports groups of up to four, with drop-in join once you've watched the mission's opening cutscene.
  • Palicoes are your solo companions and yes, they can wear tiny hats. This is important.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Twenty hours with a sword and shield and no regrets.

I picked the sword-and-shield because it seemed forgiving, learned exactly one combo, and had a great time for the first twenty hours. Then the monsters grew bigger than my patience. I kept logging on for co-op nights though, because my palico's outfit is worth the drive. That's a completely legitimate reason to play, and I'll argue it with anyone.

02

The Descent Guy

The support role is the real main character.

I picked the horn, learned the note charts, and turned into a walking buff dispenser. I know the elemental weakness of every monster in the base game and I can tell you which fight is the Rathalos 'actually you're wrong' fight. I build sets. I post them in the group chat at midnight. The other two ignore me and they're better for it.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Sixty hours, main story done, endgame skipped.

I got about sixty hours in over a season, finished the main story, and didn't touch the endgame. That's the correct dose for most dads. The hunts land beautifully, the co-op sessions are exactly the right length, and the menus are the price of admission. I'd tell you to bring a friend, and to pick the same weapon.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Groups of two to four who want hunt nights on a rotating schedule
  • Anyone who liked Dark Souls's boss-first design but wanted less dying
  • People who enjoy learning one weapon deeply and forever

Dad caveats

  • The menus and crafting UI are genuinely rough by modern standards
  • Solo is doable, but the game shines with a party of two or more
  • You will spend an evening on a Palico armour set. Everyone does. Accept it.

Receipts

Weekly four-player co-op for around three months on PC, plus one of us playing solo through the base story to check the campaign flow.
