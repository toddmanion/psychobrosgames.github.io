The thirty-second version

Miniatures strategy where gravity does the damage.

Finally, a strategy game where knocking things over counts as planning.

We haven't played it, but Table Tactics replaces invisible arithmetic with visible consequences, and that's a real design idea. Weight, shape, position and momentum decide whether a miniature stays upright, giving every move the clarity of a tabletop stunt and the possibility of an unexpected chain reaction. The Descent guy called it before the trailer ended.

Okay, what is this thing?

The board is a tabletop covered in collectible miniatures. Archers, catapults, mages, traps, marbles and other things that shouldn't be legal push instead of subtract; they apply actual force until something falls over. That's the whole win condition, and we love it. We haven't played it — we're reading the store page during lunch — and one of us is already sketching angle-of-attack diagrams.

Because weight and shape matter, building a roster is partly about physical properties, which is a lovely sentence. Coins earned through play buy and trade miniatures, so the physics puzzle sits on top of a collection layer that gives you a reason to keep prying open packs. It's the first game in ages we've all agreed to try, sight unseen.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • You win by knocking the other side's miniatures over, not by draining an HP bar.
  • Unit weight, shape, position, and applied force all matter to the outcome.
  • Coins support buying and trading miniatures to build your own roster.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Falling over is the clearest damage number in games.

I have never once understood a hit-point calculation, and now I don't have to. You aim, you push, the thing falls or it doesn't. That's a whole game explained in ten seconds. A lucky ricochet is going to be one of the funniest things I see all year, provided the aiming controls don't fight me.

02

The Descent Guy

Physics makes positioning matter twice over.

I've been asking for this since roughly forever. A unit's tactical role and its physical body both belong in the plan, and the game rewards setup, angle, force and roster construction without hiding the result behind a spreadsheet. If it holds up, the other two aren't going to hear about anything else from me for a month.

03

Normal-ish Dad

This is going to generate arguments.

I clocked it immediately: visible outcomes make the game easy to watch, easy to shout about, and easy to remember afterwards. Provided turns stay brisk, Table Tactics could work as both a solo strategy commitment and a reliable pass-the-controller party pick. I'm already saying words like 'Wednesday.'

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Tabletop players who enjoy measuring angles and consequences
  • Strategy fans tired of opaque damage formulas
  • Game-night groups who like to shout at physics

Dad caveats

  • How precise the aiming controls are with small forces
  • Whether physics stays consistent across weird unit shapes
  • How buying and trading miniatures affects roster balance
