The thirty-second version

One more run. We swear.

It's a deckbuilder. That's an actual sentence we say to our wives now.

Slay the Spire is the game we've collectively re-installed the most times over the past few years. It's genuinely small — you draw cards, play cards, do damage, don't die — and the depth comes from how sharply every relic and elite bends the run. Play it once and you'll understand every deckbuilder released since.

Okay, what is this thing?

Ironclad, a big man with a sword, walks into a spire. He has a Strike, a Defend, and roughly nine hundred bad decisions ahead of him. You pick the Strike. Then the elite fight rolls a boss relic and you're suddenly playing a completely different game, because your Strikes also heal now, or your Defends also stun. That's the whole trick.

The four characters play like four different games. Ironclad stacks strength and swings until things end. Silent poisons them from the inside. Defect stores energy into orbs that fire on their own. Watcher slips in and out of stances that do math you have to explain to your friends. Every run is roughly an hour, every choice is real, and the Ascension levels take a good game and turn it into a graduate course.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Four playable characters, each with their own decks, relics and terrible ideas.
  • Twenty Ascension levels for people whose brains work incorrectly.
  • One of the games that basically invented the modern single-player deckbuilder subgenre.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

I play Ironclad. Just Ironclad. Forever.

I picked the Ironclad on my first run, made a deck that basically does one thing well, and haven't once tried another character. I've beaten the Heart. I don't care that there are three other characters. I call them the wrong characters. My deck is called 'the good one.'

02

The Descent Guy

The purest math game masquerading as a card game.

I spent an evening working out expected damage curves for Silent's poison deck at Ascension 12 and had a genuinely religious moment when I realised strength synergy on Ironclad broke my own model. I've got a colour-coded list of relics ranked by 'do I take this if I already have Runic Pyramid.' Apparently I'm everyone's favourite person to lose to.

03

Normal-ish Dad

The perfect midweek game.

A run takes an hour, more or less. You can quit in the middle. Nobody's waiting for you. There's no season pass, no daily login, no online lobby. It's just a small, sharp game I can dip into after the kids are down and put down when my wife walks past. I've played hundreds of runs and I wouldn't describe any of them as wasted time.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who thinks card games are for accountants and would like to be corrected
  • People whose evening ends when a small hand appears in the doorway
  • Fans of roguelikes who don't want their runs to take four hours

Dad caveats

  • You will lose runs to a spider in Act 1 and it will feel personal
  • Ascension 20 is not for the well-adjusted
  • You'll spend real hours on the character select screen, staring
