The thirty-second version

The best notebook we've filled in years.

Real estate has never made us this happy or this confused.

Blue Prince is the puzzle game that took over our group chat for a full month. It looks polite and then it eats your brain in the nicest possible way, one room at a time. Committing to it means committing to a physical notebook, and honestly that's part of the deal we signed up for.

Okay, what is this thing?

Every morning at Mt. Holly you wake up, you pick a door, and you place a room. The mansion redraws itself, you find a clue about a bathtub you didn't know existed, and then the day ends and everything resets. We spent our first week losing badly and pretending it wasn't personal. Then one of us cracked and now there's a shared spreadsheet none of the wives are allowed to see.

What makes it work is that everything is a puzzle inside a puzzle. The room draft feels like a light strategy game and then you realise the mansion has an actual mystery underneath it, and that mystery cares about details you noted on day three and forgot about by day nine. The game trusts you to keep up, which is flattering until you don't.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Every morning redraws Mt. Holly from a fresh draft of rooms, so no two mansions are the same.
  • There's a mystery underneath the mystery, and it's the kind that rewards a real paper notebook.
  • Absolutely a single-player game, despite spawning at least three private chats in our house.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The mansion doesn't want me, and I agree.

I opened Blue Prince, drew a hallway that led to nothing, and closed Blue Prince. On night two I tried again with a beer and the same thing happened. I'm not the target audience for a game that asks me to write things down. I am, however, the target audience for laughing at the other two writing things down, and I've been eating well.

02

The Descent Guy

The best drafting-and-notes game in years.

I haven't been this happy since I first discovered pen-and-paper mysteries. Blue Prince is a drafting game inside a deduction game inside a mansion that keeps reshuffling itself, and I treat it like a job with better hours. I was chasing Room 46 while my eggs went cold. I'd do it again.

03

Normal-ish Dad

It's a marathon, and worth every hour if you're in the mood.

A single day at Mt. Holly is a bite-sized run, which is friendly for a tired brain. The whole mystery is not bite-sized, and that's fair. I knocked off a couple of days a night for weeks and never resented the pace. If you can't stand keeping notes in a real notebook you'll have a bad time, but if you can, this is the best puzzle game we've played all year.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who owns at least one graph-paper notebook they're proud of
  • Puzzle solvers who want a mystery big enough to text a friend about
  • People whose brains are itching for a game to bully them nicely

Dad caveats

  • It really does want you to take notes; no notes, no dice
  • The full mystery is a long haul, not a weekend
  • You will start describing your actual house in terms of doors and gems

Receipts

Played over about a month on PC by two of us, comparing notes in a shared doc that has since become embarrassing to look at. The third dropped in occasionally to laugh.
