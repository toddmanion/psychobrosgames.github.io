The thirty-second version

Monster-taming with the lights turned down.

Catch monsters, fight oblivion, try to remember which dad named the frog Terry.

Voidsayer is the darker, meaner cousin of the monster-tamer we all associate with elementary school. A hundred-plus tameable creatures, gear on top, evolution on top of that, and a world that isn't waiting for you to catch up. The tone alone is enough to make us curious. Whether it stays interesting depends on the roster having actual role differences, not a hundred copies of the same fire dog.

Okay, what is this thing?

Watching the trailer, the first thing you notice is the mood. The Abyss is eating the world. You are not, in this game, cheerfully collecting friends. You are recruiting soldiers for a fight the world is already losing. That is a solid premise. It's the kind of setup that makes a hundred-plus creature roster feel like a purpose instead of a checklist. The question is how many of those creatures actually feel different in a fight.

Under the tone is a genuinely deep tamer. Gear on every monster. Evolution paths on top of that. Procedural events and challenges across an interconnected map so you can't just memorize a single dominant team. That's a good frame; it turns team-building into a real, changing problem. Our only worry is the grind. If evolving and gearing three teams for three encounter types requires a spreadsheet and an evening we don't have, this becomes a game we admire from a distance.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • The roster runs to more than 100 tameable monsters, per the studio.
  • Monsters can equip gear and go through evolutions.
  • Procedural events and challenges vary the interconnected world map.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

Roster size is a promise and a threat.

I'm fine catching monsters. I'm not fine reading a menu of gear stats on a hundred of them. What I want is a filter that says 'this is who you should use for the next thing' and then lets me get on with it. If the game hands me roles and recommendations early, I can enjoy the collection. If it dumps a spreadsheet on me, I'll pick five monsters and never touch the rest.

02

The Descent Guy

Roster construction should punish universal squads.

This is my catnip. I want encounters that break a lazy party: a boss that ignores your one killer strategy, an area whose weather flips your damage type, a procedural event that penalizes the exact combo you've been leaning on. If the game actually forces roster rebuilds, gear and evolution turn into real tools. If it doesn't, a small set of monsters solve everything and the hundred is decoration.

03

Normal-ish Dad

A darker tone alone can carry a long campaign.

Most monster-tamers still read like a Saturday morning cartoon, and this one doesn't. That mood is enough to get me interested. What I want next is procedural events that actually stay memorable. If every random encounter is the same three problems, the campaign goes flat. If a run gives me three or four moments worth texting the other two about, I'll finish the whole thing.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Monster-tamer fans who want gear and evolution to be real levers
  • RPG players comfortable rebuilding a party for a new problem
  • Anyone who thinks the genre could use a lot less sunshine

Dad caveats

  • Menu clarity when your roster crosses into three digits
  • Grinding needed to swap in an alternate team
  • Whether procedural events give you memorable stories, not just alternate stats
