The thirty-second version

The rip and tear renaissance.

I have not been this loud at midnight since the children were born.

DOOM (2016) is what happens when id decides shooters can be fun again. You move like a track star, you tear demons in half with your bare hands, and the whole game is scored by Mick Gordon at a volume that will get you divorced. It's the tightest single-player shooter any of us has played in a decade and we cannot recommend it more strongly.

Okay, what is this thing?

You start the game handcuffed to a slab. Ten seconds later you've smashed a zombie's head into the floor and picked up a shotgun off a corpse. The tutorial pop-up says something. You don't read it. Nobody reads it. You just start moving, and about fourteen hours later you will emerge blinking into daylight and realise the phrase 'work-life balance' meant something to you once. It's an assault. In a good way.

The core loop is the whole reason. Demons drop health only when you glory-kill them, ammo only when you chainsaw them, and armour only when you use the flame belch. The game is teaching you to keep moving, be aggressive, and never, ever hide behind cover. It's a great thirty-minute session game with a fantastic campaign attached, and yes, that music will live in your head rent-free forever.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • A hard reboot of the DOOM name that pretends the last two decades of shooter fashion never happened.
  • The core loop punishes cover and rewards aggression; glory kills refill health, chainsaws refill ammo.
  • The soundtrack is by Mick Gordon, which is why your subwoofer needs a breather between rooms.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The shooter I actually finished. I didn't see it coming either.

I don't usually last two hours in a shooter campaign. I sat down with DOOM, held the shotgun, ran into a demon, and suddenly I was the one telling everybody else to pause because I wanted 'one more arena'. I don't fully understand the upgrade menus and I don't need to. I just move and shoot and grin.

02

The Descent Guy

Health from glory kills. Ammo from chainsaw. It's a whole doctrine.

I spotted the design straight away: the game teaches you to be aggressive by making cover a losing move and executions a healing move. The chainsaw as a resource valve is the elegant part. I also drew a Descent comparison inside the first thirty minutes, because momentum-based movement drags me back to 1995 no matter what genre we're in. I'm not wrong.

03

Normal-ish Dad

Perfectly cut into thirty-minute chunks. School-night gold.

The campaign breaks naturally into arenas that resolve in twenty to thirty minutes. I played it in bite-sized bursts on PC over two months and never once wished I had four uninterrupted hours. That's a rare thing in a modern shooter. I also keep the master volume at seventy percent, because my youngest can hear a fly two rooms over.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Fans of the original DOOM who thought the genre had lost its way
  • Anyone who wants a shooter that respects thirty minutes at a time
  • Dads with a good pair of headphones and understanding neighbours

Dad caveats

  • The music will move your ribcage; be nice to sleepers upstairs
  • Some of the rune trials are annoying spikes in the flow
  • The upgrade menus are more than you'd expect from a game this fast

Receipts

One full campaign each on PC across a couple of months, plus revisits when we wanted to remember why we like shooters at all.
