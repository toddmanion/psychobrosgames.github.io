The thirty-second version

Yes. Still. Buy it now.

Turns out the scariest thing at 45 is the footsteps of a very tall man in a hat.

Capcom took a 1998 tank-controls classic and rebuilt it into one of the best horror games any of us has finished. It's tight, it's mean, and it uses its budget like every dollar had a name. Two campaigns, both worth doing, and the second time through it's a different game because you know where the herbs live.

Okay, what is this thing?

You walk into a police station that is somehow also a mansion designed by a man who really liked chess pieces. The lights buzz. There's a body in the lobby that will absolutely stand back up. Somewhere upstairs a very quiet, very heavy man in a hat has just started walking, and he does not need to run, because you are the one with the ammo problem.

What makes this remake sing is that everything in your inventory is a decision. Grenades, boards, herbs, keys, the pistol you probably shouldn't have used, all fighting for the same handful of slots. Enemies don't stay dead unless you actually kill them, which for a zombie takes more shots than you have. Save rooms feel like coming up for air. It's horror as spreadsheet, and we mean that as a compliment.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Both Leon and Claire have their own campaigns, and yes, you should play both.
  • It runs on the RE Engine, which is why the fog looks like fog and the shirts look like shirts.
  • Mr. X shows up partway through the police station and does not care about your item box logic.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The scariest game I've finished since our twenties.

I got about ninety minutes in, met Mr. X for the first time, and quit until the other two peer-pressured me back a week later. To my credit, I did finish it. I also spent the entire police station holding my breath, and every save room typewriter felt like a small win I was allowed to celebrate out loud.

02

The Descent Guy

Small inventory, a hundred decisions, no wrong answers, plenty of wrong answers.

I treated it like a resource puzzle from minute one. Grenades saved for the sewers, gunpowder combos worked out on paper, the shotgun kept in the box until it earned a slot. Then Mr. X kicked the door down and none of it mattered for about eleven minutes. I said, out loud, 'this is good design.'

03

Normal-ish Dad

The kind of horror game we actually finish.

Eight to ten hours per campaign, roughly. I can knock out Leon over three evenings if the kids cooperate, then do Claire the next weekend and get a slightly different story for my trouble. It doesn't drag. It doesn't pad. It's the rare AAA game that respects that we have work in the morning.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who wanted to feel this scared but forgot horror was allowed to be this scared
  • Fans of the 1998 original who are willing to admit the tank controls were rough
  • Dads who want a horror game that finishes in a week, not a season

Dad caveats

  • Mr. X is a real presence and he does not respect your podcast schedule
  • You will absolutely die because you tried to conserve shotgun shells
  • Some of the puzzles are still the same medallion nonsense as 1998, which we love, but

Receipts

Two of us finished both Leon and Claire on PC across a few weeks; the third bailed after the sewers and has been threatening to come back for a year. Details checked against the game's official Steam listing.
