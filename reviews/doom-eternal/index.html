The thirty-second version

A metal album you can play.

Our doctor said no juggling. We didn't tell him about the chainsaw part.

DOOM Eternal is the loudest, fastest, most gleefully complicated first-person shooter of its generation, and it does not care whether you keep up. It's a symphony of resource management dressed up as a demon-punching festival. Once you get the loop — chainsaw for ammo, glory kill for health, flame belch for armour — it's the best FPS combat since the original DOOM.

Okay, what is this thing?

You are the Slayer. You are on Earth. It has been overrun by demons who are, honestly, a bit whingy about it. You have a shotgun that shoots a hook, a chainsaw that respawns to give you ammo, a flamethrower on your shoulder that makes armour, and an ice bomb that freezes things. The tutorial hands you all of these in about twenty minutes, and then the game says, right, keep up.

The combat is a puzzle. Enemies drop specific things when killed specific ways. The chainsaw is your ammo economy. Glory kills are your health economy. Flame Belch is your armour economy. If you stand still you die, if you use the wrong weapon on the wrong demon you run out of shells, and if you get it right it's the most kinetic thing in the medium. It's not for everyone. It's very much for some of us.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • The combat loop is built around dropped resources — ammo from chainsaw, health from glory kills, armour from Flame Belch.
  • Every gun has meaningful secondary fire modes, and swapping between them mid-fight is the whole game.
  • There are platforming sections, and yes, some people would like to speak with id Software's manager about them.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The one shooter I genuinely wanted to keep playing.

I don't normally play first-person shooters. I put it on the easier difficulty, treated the chainsaw as my emotional support tool, and had the best time I've had with a shooter in a decade. I still don't use the ice bomb correctly. I'm pretty sure the Flame Belch is called something else. I finished it. I was proud of myself. The other two were proud of me too, and I've decided to just accept that.

02

The Descent Guy

The first FPS since the original that's actually about thinking.

I treat every combat arena as a systems puzzle. Which demon do I kill for what I need next? Which weapon gets me the drop? I built an actual mental flowchart and then ran the whole campaign on Ultra-Violence like it was my job. I want you to know the ballista is criminally underused. I'm not wrong.

03

Normal-ish Dad

You cannot play it for four hours in a row and remain a person.

It's loud. It's fast. It's genuinely tiring. Ninety-minute sessions are the sweet spot; any longer and my hands ache and my neighbours know exactly which fight I'm in. I finished it in about six weeks of evenings, and I'd describe the last mission as a personal test I passed by two hit points. Which is exactly right.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who wanted their shooter with genuine rules
  • People who miss when shooters were about movement and not cover
  • Dads whose kids are old enough to sleep through a chainsaw

Dad caveats

  • It's exhausting, and that's on purpose
  • The platforming is genuinely divisive
  • Marauders exist and we will not be discussing them further

Receipts

All three of us played through on PC, two on the middle difficulty and one on Ultra-Violence, mostly in ninety-minute weekend chunks. Details checked against the game's official Steam listing.
