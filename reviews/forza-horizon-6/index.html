The thirty-second version

Still the best chill racer going.

Bought a kei van in-game, thought about it for a week, did not buy one in real life.

Forza Horizon 6 is exactly what we needed after a long week: a huge, gorgeous open-world racer that lets you drive fast on beautiful roads and never punishes you for wanting to just cruise. Japan looks unreal. The cars sound right. And the moment-to-moment driving is still the most relaxing thing Playground Games make.

Okay, what is this thing?

The first hour of Horizon 6 has us tearing down a coastal road at sunset in a very silly car, with cherry blossom on the windscreen, and one of us saying 'this is why I bought a graphics card.' Japan is a fantastic Horizon map: winding mountain roads, dense city sections, sleepy fishing towns you can just accidentally drift through. It's the biggest one they've made and it feels the most like a place.

Under the hood it's still the Horizon formula, and that's a good thing. Race, festival, freeway, roam, buy a car you already own in a different colour, repeat. The driving model is the friendlier end of the sim spectrum: you can turn assists off if you want to hurt yourself, or leave them on and just have a nice time. This is the ideal Sunday-morning game.

Why it escaped the group chat

  • Set in Japan, and per the marketing there are over 550 real-world cars in the roster.
  • Latest entry from Playground Games, who have been making Horizon games for over a decade.
  • Fully released on Windows, controller very much recommended.

Three dads enter

01

Dad Who Skipped the Tutorial

The only racing game I'll actually finish a session in.

I turn the driving assists on, point the car at the horizon, and drive to podcasts. That's the whole loop for me, and Horizon is designed for exactly that guy. I don't do time trials. I don't tune. I drive, I collect a barn find, and I'm asleep by ten with a small smile. The dream.

02

The Descent Guy

The tuning depth is still deeper than it needs to be, in a good way.

I went straight into the tuning menu and started shaving hundredths off lap times. Horizon's underlying model is friendly-sim, and I think that's exactly right for a game you want to be able to zone out to and also nerd out in when you're ready. I've got a Miata that's going to haunt our online lobbies.

03

Normal-ish Dad

It's a very big game, and you don't have to do most of it.

The map is enormous, the events are endless, and none of it demands your attention. I finished the intro on a Saturday morning, then dipped in for twenty-minute drives across the week and never once felt behind. That's the trick with Horizon: it's a game you can put down for a month and pick up like you never left.

Who it is for and what to watch

Invite these people

  • Anyone who wants to drive down a Japanese mountain road at sunset and cry a bit
  • Racing fans who like a chill sim floor, not a punishing one
  • People who need a game they can put down for a month and return to easily

Dad caveats

  • It's a genuinely huge game; you can drown in the menus if you let yourself
  • Online is fun but the AI drivers are still the ones you'll do the most driving with
  • You will 100% download a wildly inappropriate custom livery, no matter your age

Receipts

Played solo on PC with a wheel for two of us and a controller for the third, across evenings and Saturday mornings when the kids were watching cartoons.
